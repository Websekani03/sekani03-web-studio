My Study Environment

Learning coding while working as a Nurse has forced me to become more disciplined with my time. After long shifts, especially on difficult days, it would be easier to rest and postpone learning. Instead, I try to remain consistent by sitting down to practice even when I feel mentally tired. My goal is not just to watch tutorials, but to build real understanding through repetition, problem solving, and daily effort.

Coding environment is simple but focused. Most nights I study with my laptop after work, often practicing for several hours while reviewing concepts I learned earlier in the day. On days I'm on night shifts, I take my laptop to work and continue practicing with it. I am learning to stay patient with myself during confusing moments and to keep improving gradually instead of expecting perfection immediately. Every small improvement gives me more confidence and keeps me moving forward toward becoming a developer.

Daily Developer Mindset

Becoming a developer is teaching me how to think differently. Instead of giving up when something breaks, I am learning to slow down, inspect the problem, and solve it step by step. Debugging has shown me that mistakes are normal and that improvement comes from understanding why errors happen instead of avoiding them completely.

Consistency matters more than motivation in coding. Some days feel productive and exciting while other days feel slow and frustrating. What matters is continuing to practice anyway. Over time, repeated efforts build confidence, sharper thinking, and the ability to create things independently. I am beginning to understand that real developers are built through patience, repetition , and continuous learning.
